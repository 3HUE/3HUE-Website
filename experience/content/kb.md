## The model: a top-down approach in three layers
1. GRC Management Layer — establishes "tone at the top" by aligning business priorities with risk, compliance, and security governance. Sets policy direction and drives strategic accountability. Delivered as six managed programs.
2. Secure Engineering & Architecture (ISG-SEA) — translates GRC insights into actionable architecture improvements: advisory, design, and hands-on engineering to close control gaps and future-proof enterprise systems. Disciplines: Architecture, Build, Secure, Operate.
3. Security Operations (ISG-OPS) — managed SOC capabilities form the operational backbone: modular, US-based, real-time threat monitoring, incident response, and compliance reporting.

## The six GRC managed programs (12-month sprints; effort estimated per workload)
- Virtual CISO (vCISO): experienced security executives integrate with your team to shape security strategy, drive decision-making, and oversee compliance initiatives. Board-facing cybersecurity expertise; strengthens trust among customers and stakeholders; enhances leadership confidence and accountability; can capably manage M&A activity. Effort: 68–164 hours annual commit; ~90 hours annual SOC 2 maintenance (CISO Support option). Leadership options: Option 1 No Virtual CISO (you have a full-time CISO/experienced leader); Option 2 CISO Support (build an internal CISO capability); Option 3 Fractional CISO (staff augmentation for the CISO role). vCISOs are former CISO/CIO professionals; also available flexibly as-needed for overflow or to mentor an incumbent CISO.
- Information Security Program (ISG-ISP): build and manage a formal information security program aligned to frameworks like SCF, NIST, or ISO to ensure consistent controls and audit readiness. Formalizes policies, roles, and responsibilities; drives consistency in security operations and governance; supports compliance with regulatory standards and contractual obligations; scales with growing regulatory or customer demands. Effort: 96–244 hours annual; ~120 hours SOC 2 maintenance. Outcome claim: implement a mature SOC 2, PCI-DSS, or CMMC compliant program in under 6 months.
- Risk Management Program (ISG-RMP): establishes a risk management function to identify, prioritize, and mitigate security risks and foster a proactive, risk-informed culture. Prioritizes security investments on actual risk; supports risk registers, assessments, and remediation tracking; improves readiness for regulatory reviews and partner due diligence. Effort: 60–472 hours annual; ~144 hours SOC 2.
- Cyber-Incident Response Program (ISG-CIRP): structured response planning and incident command services for coordinated containment and recovery, including annual simulations and tabletop exercises. Accelerated readiness for high-severity incidents; clear roles and escalation paths; rapid, coordinated containment and recovery. Effort: 70–120 hours annual; ~100 hours SOC 2.
- Vendor Compliance Program (ISG-VCP): manages third-party risk by aligning vendor practices with organizational risk tolerance, contractual controls, and regulatory expectations. Improved visibility and control over third-party risk; audit-ready documentation of vendor assurance; reduced exposure through proactive vendor oversight and attack-surface posture validation. Effort: 120–365 hours annual; ~240 hours SOC 2.
- Security Compliance Services (ISG-SCS): sustained audit support and regulatory alignment using change monitoring and continuous policy compliance tracking. Sustained audit support; regulatory risk reduction using continuous change detection; audit stakeholder preparedness. Effort: 60–120 hours annual; ~60 hours SOC 2. Outcome claim: up to 60% reduction in audit preparation time.

## How services are delivered: three-step integrated engagement
Step 1 — Govern & plan (align on deliverables): Client Operating Plan defines and authorizes service delivery; operational workload retainers define frequency and effort of GRC workstreams; performance reporting metrics; Shared Responsibility Matrix; ISG-GRC Operational Runbooks define the operating project plan, escalations and service levels (indicators of compromise, attack, exposure), and a calendar schedule of workloads.
Step 2 — GRC operations (deploy GRC operational systems): GRC operations hosted on your Microsoft 365 tenant (M365 GRC Management System; an option for organizations managing no more than ~5,000 information assets) or on your enterprise GRC system. Define the control baseline tailored to your risk and regulatory environment. Operationalize managed programs: secure share, request workflows, client portal, risk register, vendor profiles, action plans, BI tools. Continuous change detection across systems with actionable Indicators of Risk (IoR); real-time detection of unauthorized changes and misconfigurations; risk-posture change notifications; multi-platform inspectors for Azure, AWS, M365 and popular cloud/SaaS; endpoint agents and cloud API inspectors across on-premises and cloud. Also: GRC system configuration and maintenance of an existing enterprise GRC platform.
Step 3 — Security operations (deploy managed SOC services): 24/7 managed security operations via partner Whitedog SOC: Managed Detection & Response (MDR), Internet Threat Protection (phishing, digital masquerading, threat intel), Managed Zero-Trust Network Access, Threat Hunting & Red Team Testing (included without additional fees). RiskOps integrated; optimized incident response; audit readiness (incident response never creates non-compliance); 24/7 real-time monitoring; fast incident response from detection to client notification within 15 minutes; composable protection refined with MITRE-style evaluations; 50+ commercial and open-source tools. Supported platforms: Windows desktop/server (incl. legacy XP/2003/2008), macOS, major Linux distributions, Kubernetes (self-managed, AWS, Azure), Citrix, VMware, Hyper-V, VirtualBox.

## AiVRIC (the risk intelligence platform) and Client Vision
AiVRIC is a risk intelligence platform made by AiVRIC (aivric.com). 3HUE uses AiVRIC to monitor client estates and to power Client Vision, the customer interface; other consultancies can use AiVRIC to run their own service models. Inside 3HUE's delivery, AiVRIC is the shared data layer under every program: findings from every program and from the SOC converge into one posture, one set of priorities, visible progress, and decisions executives can act on. Client Vision has four panels: Posture (unified cloud, AI, and control posture across the estate), Priorities (findings ranked by business impact, not just severity), Progress (treatments, SLAs, and audit readiness tracked in one place), Decisions (board-ready readouts and AI-generated narratives).
