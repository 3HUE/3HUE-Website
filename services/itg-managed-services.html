Technical Depth

Program component details

Summaries of each managed program component and leadership option.

Enterprise Architecture & Intelligence (EAI)

Establishes reference architectures, technology standards, and a forward-looking roadmap aligned to business strategy and modernization goals.

  • Defines target-state and reference architectures across applications, data, and infrastructure.
  • Establishes technology standards, patterns, and guardrails for consistent decision-making.
  • Maintains a living technology roadmap tied to business priorities.
  • Reduces technical debt and architecture drift across the portfolio.

Portfolio & Project Management (PPM)

Brings structured governance to technology investment, sequencing, and delivery so modernization initiatives land on time and on budget.

  • Prioritizes and sequences initiatives against capacity, risk, and business value.
  • Establishes stage-gate governance, milestones, and delivery reporting.
  • Provides portfolio-level visibility into cost, status, and dependencies.
  • Improves executive confidence in modernization timelines and spend.

IT Infrastructure & Operations (ITIO)

Modernizes and operates the core infrastructure — cloud, network, endpoint, and service management — that everything else depends on.

  • Assesses and modernizes cloud, network, and endpoint environments.
  • Establishes service management practices aligned to ITIL 4 principles.
  • Improves reliability, performance, and cost efficiency of core infrastructure.
  • Provides a stable foundation for data, AI, and application modernization.

Data & AI Management (DAIM)

Builds governed data platforms and AI enablement so analytics and AI initiatives scale without introducing unmanaged risk.

  • Establishes data governance, quality, and classification standards.
  • Designs data platform architecture to support analytics and AI workloads.
  • Defines AI use-case intake, model lifecycle, and oversight controls.
  • Aligns data and AI initiatives to enterprise architecture and compliance requirements.

Business Continuity Management (BCM)

Establishes continuity planning, disaster recovery, and resilience testing so critical operations withstand disruption.

  • Performs business impact analysis to identify critical processes and dependencies.
  • Develops and maintains continuity and disaster recovery plans.
  • Runs tabletop exercises and recovery testing on a defined cadence.
  • Aligns continuity practices to ISO 22301 and industry expectations.

Virtual CIO / Fractional CIO (vCIO)

Provides embedded executive technology leadership to guide architecture, infrastructure, data, and continuity decisions.

  • Provides board- and executive-level technology guidance and reporting.
  • Bridges business strategy and technology execution across programs.
  • Offers flexible coverage without the cost of a full-time executive hire.
  • Builds internal capability through mentoring and knowledge transfer.

3HUE's Cross-Framework Meta-Model

Every ITG capability maps once to the Digital Maturity Paradigm (DMP)

The DMP Framework is 3HUE's unified capability model for enterprise architecture and technology governance — used much like USR is used for GRC management. Instead of tracking ITIL 4, TOGAF, DAMA-DMBOK, COBIT 2019, and other standards in parallel, every ITG Managed EA Program capability is mapped once to DMP, then cross-walked to whichever frameworks your organization is accountable to.

DMP spans 3 pillars, 8 core dimensions, and 184 defined capabilities on a shared 1–5 maturity scale — giving architecture, infrastructure, data & AI, and continuity teams one common language for current-state and target-state.

What DMP Adds
  • ● One capability model cross-mapped to ITIL 4, TOGAF, DAMA-DMBOK, COBIT 2019, ISO 22301, and NIST CSF
  • ● Shared 1–5 maturity scale across infrastructure, data & AI, and continuity
  • ● 3 pillars, 8 core dimensions, 184 defined capabilities
  • ● Evidence-driven roadmap: Assess → Align → Implement → Measure → Optimize
  • ● Verifiable maturity credentials for board and audit reporting
Your existing frameworks stay in force. Adopting DMP doesn't replace TOGAF, ITIL 4, COBIT, or DAMA-DMBOK obligations — it gives you one place to track progress against all of them at once.
